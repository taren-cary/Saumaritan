The "Generate Again" Loop & AI HallucinationsYour idea of letting the auditor run the AI multiple times to catch missed transactions makes total sense from a UI perspective, but it exposes a technical risk: AI context windows and non-determinism.If the AI is missing transactions on the first pass, it is likely due to the size of the transaction list overloading the prompt context, or the temperature being too high.The Regulatory Fix:An auditor cannot rely on a tool where the logic changes or "catches new things" just by hitting a button on the exact same data. If an AI missing something on Run 1 suddenly catches it on Run 2, the auditor can't legally defend their methodology.The Solution: Don't have the AI look at a massive list of transactions all at once to generate findings. Instead, have the AI process transactions row-by-row (or in small batches) upon upload to assign a Risk Score or Flag Status (e.g., "High Risk: Missing Vendor Data").Then, the "Generate Report" button simply compiles the already-flagged transactions into the PDF format. This makes the system deterministic, defensible, and drastically cheaper on API token costs.2. Refining the Transaction Status CategoriesIn a Single Audit, "Allowable" and "Unallowable" have strict legal definitions, and there is a middle ground you need to account for. The Date Range Picker & The "Fiscal Year" GuardrailYour date range picker is an excellent feature, but you should explicitly tie it to the Nonprofit’s Fiscal Year, not just arbitrary dates.As established, a Single Audit is strictly bounded by the organization's 12-month fiscal calendar. If an auditor selects a date range that spans 14 months or crosses the nonprofit's fiscal year-end (e.g., December 31st), the resulting PDF report will be legally invalid for a Single Audit submission.App Feature Idea: When creating the "Audit Project," force the auditor to enter the Fiscal Year (e.g., FY 2025: Jan 1, 2025 - Dec 31, 2025). Then, auto-set the date picker to that exact range so they don't accidentally pull in transactions from the wrong audit period.4. How the PDF Report Evolution Should WorkYou mentioned that hitting "Generate Again" will append new findings to the initial report.Instead of generating a new PDF or appending text to an old one programmatically, think of the PDF as a live dashboard export.The AI flags transactions.The auditor reviews them on screen.The app maintains a living list of "Active Findings."The auditor only hits "Export PDF" once at the very end of the engagement when the final negotiations with the nonprofit are complete.This prevents the auditor from having 15 versions of a PDF floating around their local hard drive, which is a major data-integrity risk during a government peer review.Summary of the Ideal UI State MachineUpload: Transactions come in $\rightarrow$ State = Pending Review.Analyze: Auditor triggers analysis $\rightarrow$ AI changes risky rows to Questioned.The Lock Mechanism: The "Generate Report" button shouldn't lock based on the AI finding nothing; it should lock when Questioned count = 0.Resolution: The auditor moves Questioned items to either Cleared or Disallowed.Finalization: Once all transactions are either Cleared, Disallowed, or Pending Review (if outside the date range), the auditor exports the final, clean Schedule of Findings and Questioned Costs PDF.You've built a remarkably clean, intuitive loop here. By treating the AI as an initial flagging assistant rather than the final report writer, you'll make the tool incredibly defensible for an auditor's strict compliance standards.
